Probability and Gaussian basics
1. Joint, marginal and Bayes
x - One observation, such as an image or a word feature.
y - A class label or conditioning category.
p(x,y) - Joint probability of x and y occurring together.
p(x|y) - Likelihood of observation x within class y.
p(y) - Prior probability of class y before seeing x.
p(x) - Evidence: total probability of observing x.
p(y|x) - Posterior probability of class y after seeing x.
k in the denominator - Index over all possible classes.

2. Expectations and variance
X - A random variable; x is one possible value of X.
f(X) - The quantity whose probability-weighted average we want.
E[...] - Expectation, or a probability-weighted average.
E with a hat - A finite-sample estimate of the expectation.
N - Number of observed or sampled values in the average.
i - Sample index; x_i is the i-th observation.
Var(X) - Expected squared spread around the mean of X.
E[X^2] - Expected square, not the square of the expectation.
v - Variance: squared spread.
σ (sigma) - Standard deviation: square root of variance.
a, b - Constant scaling coefficients applied to random variables.
E in aX+bE - A second random variable, distinct from expectation E[...].

3. Gaussian density
N(z;μ,v) - Gaussian density evaluated at z, with mean μ and variance v.
z - The value at which the density is evaluated.
μ (mu) - Mean: the center of the Gaussian distribution.
v > 0 - Variance: controls the Gaussian spread.
π (pi) - The constant approximately 3.14159.
e - Euler's number, approximately 2.71828.
exp(a) = e^a - Exponential function, inverse of natural logarithm.

Autoencoders, gradients and latent geometry
1. Encoder, decoder and a linear layer
x - Input observation or row vector of input features.
z - Latent code produced by the encoder.
f_phi - Encoder function with trainable parameters phi.
phi (φ) - Collection of encoder weights and biases.
g_theta - Decoder function with trainable parameters theta.
theta (θ) - Collection of decoder/model weights and biases.
x_hat - Reconstructed input produced by the decoder.
u - Output feature vector of the affine layer.
W - Trainable matrix that mixes input coordinates.
b - Trainable bias vector added after multiplication.

2. Reconstruction loss and anomaly threshold
SSE - Sum of squared reconstruction errors.
MSE - Mean squared error: SSE divided by data dimension D.
D - Number of compared data coordinates; not the GAN discriminator.
d - Coordinate index in the reconstruction vector.
x_d, x_hat_d - True and reconstructed values of coordinate d.
∂MSE/∂x_hat_d - Sensitivity of MSE to that reconstructed coordinate.
τ (tau) - Error threshold above which the input is flagged.

3. Gradient descent and chain rule
L - Scalar loss to minimize.
θ (theta) - Trainable parameter or parameter vector being updated.
η (eta) - Learning rate: size of the descent step.
grad_theta L - Vector of loss derivatives with respect to theta.
w - One trainable parameter whose derivative is needed.
u_j - Intermediate value on the j-th computational path.
∂L/∂w - Local rate of loss change when w changes.
sum over j - Add contributions from every path reaching w.

Distances, interpolation and VAE sampling
1. Retrieval distances
a, b - Feature or latent vectors being compared.
j - Coordinate index within a vector.
d(a,b) - Euclidean distance: straight-line separation.
||a|| - Euclidean vector length, square root of sum of squares.
a^T b - Dot product: multiply matching entries and sum.
T superscript - Transpose: interchange row and column orientation.
cos(a,b) - Cosine similarity: normalized dot product.

2. Latent interpolation and attribute editing
z_A, z_B - Starting and ending latent codes.
λ (lambda) - Interpolation fraction from 0 to 1.
z(λ) - Intermediate latent code to decode.
z_attribute - Code representing the attribute-bearing example.
z_base - Reference code without that attribute.
Δz (delta z) - Estimated latent direction for the attribute.
z_other - Another code to which the direction is applied.
z_edited - Resulting code after adding the attribute direction.

3. VAE encoder distribution and sample
q_phi(z|x) - Encoder's approximate latent distribution for input x.
p(z) - Prior distribution used when generating new samples.
μ - Encoder-predicted mean vector.
σ^2 or v - Encoder-predicted variance vector.
diag(σ^2) - Covariance matrix with those variances on its diagonal.
I - Identity matrix: unit variance and zero cross-covariance here.
ell (ℓ) - Log variance, ln(σ^2); not standard deviation.
ln - Natural logarithm, with base e.
σ - Standard deviation, exp(ell/2), used in sampling.
ε (epsilon) - Standard Gaussian noise drawn independently of parameters.
circle-dot (⊙) - Multiply matching vector coordinates elementwise.
z - Sampled latent vector passed to the decoder.
~ symbol - Means drawn from the distribution on its right.

KL divergence and Gaussian regularization
1. Discrete and expectation forms of KL
D_KL(P||Q) - Directional mismatch from distribution P to Q.
P_j - Probability of outcome j in the first distribution.
Q_j - Probability of outcome j in the comparison distribution.
j - Index over the discrete outcomes.
P_j / Q_j - Probability ratio whose natural log is measured.
sum over j - Add the probability-weighted contributions.
q(z), p(z) - First and comparison probability densities for latent z.
E_q - Average under the FIRST distribution q.
|| inside KL - Separates the two distributions; not a vector norm.

2. Diagonal Gaussian KL to N(0,I)
K - Total KL penalty for one encoded observation.
j - Latent-coordinate index, summed across the code.
μ_j - Mean of latent coordinate j.
v_j = σ_j^2 - Variance of latent coordinate j.
ell_j - Natural log of v_j.
exp(ell_j) - Recovers the variance v_j.
μ_j^2 - Penalty for displacement of the latent mean from zero.
v_j - 1 - ln(v_j) - Penalty for variance differing from one.

3. KL gradients
∂K/∂μ_j - KL derivative with respect to latent mean j.
∂K/∂ell_j - KL derivative with respect to latent log variance j.
μ_j - Mean value that also equals its KL derivative.
exp(ell_j)-1 - Variance minus the prior variance of one.

VAE objectives and reparameterization
1. ELBO and weighted VAE objective
ELBO - Evidence lower bound on the data log-likelihood.
q_phi(z|x) - Encoder distribution used for the latent expectation.
p_theta(x|z) - Decoder likelihood of the input given a latent code.
ln p_theta(x|z) - Reconstruction log-likelihood to maximize.
-ln p_theta(x|z) - Reconstruction negative log-likelihood to minimize.
K - KL from the encoder distribution to the latent prior.
L_VAE - Reconstruction penalty plus weighted KL penalty.
β_KL - KL coefficient; one gives the usual negative ELBO.
x, x_hat - True and reconstructed observation vectors.
s^2 - Fixed observation variance of a Gaussian decoder.
constant - Term independent of the reconstructed mean for fixed s^2.

2. Explicit pixel/latent/batch reductions
L_batch - Mean objective across the minibatch.
B - Number of observations in the minibatch.
i - Observation index within the minibatch.
D - Number of data/pixel coordinates per observation.
d - Coordinate index in that observation.
x_id, x_hat_id - True and predicted coordinate d of observation i.
K_i - KL summed over latent coordinates for observation i.
β_KL - Weight of the KL term relative to reconstruction.

3. Reparameterization derivatives
z_j - Sampled value of latent coordinate j.
μ_j - Mean of that latent coordinate.
σ_j - Standard deviation of that latent coordinate.
ε_j - Sampled standard-normal noise, held fixed during backprop.
ell_j - Log variance used to compute σ_j.
∂z_j/∂μ_j - Unit sensitivity to the latent mean.
∂z_j/∂σ_j - Sensitivity to standard deviation equals ε_j.
∂z_j/∂ell_j - Sensitivity to log variance equals half σ_j ε_j.

GAN objectives and discriminator gradients
1. The adversarial game
G - Generator mapping a latent noise vector to a fake observation.
D - Discriminator scoring how real an observation appears.
x - A real observation sampled from the training distribution.
p_data - Real-data distribution.
z - Random generator input drawn from the latent prior.
G(z) - Generated observation.
D(x), D(G(z)) - Discriminator real-probability scores on real/fake data.
V(D,G) - Value function maximized by D and minimized by G.
L_D - Discriminator minimization loss: negative value function.
L_G,sat - Saturating/minimax generator minimization loss.
L_G,NS - Non-saturating generator minimization loss.
E_x, E_z - Averages over real data and latent noise, respectively.

2. Sigmoid, BCE and its logit derivative
a - Discriminator logit before the sigmoid function.
σ(a) - Sigmoid function; here sigma does not mean standard deviation.
q - Predicted real-probability score, sigmoid(a).
y - Binary target: 1 for real, 0 for fake in a D update.
BCE(y,q) - Binary cross-entropy between target and prediction.
dq/da - Sigmoid derivative, q times one minus q.
∂BCE/∂a - Per-example logit derivative, prediction minus target.

GAN feedback, conditioning and StackGAN
1. Generator logit and parameter gradients
a - Discriminator logit for the generated observation.
q - Sigmoid(a), the fake observation's real score.
∂L_G,sat/∂a - Per-example saturating logit gradient, -q.
∂L_G,NS/∂a - Per-example non-saturating logit gradient, q-1.
θ_G - Generator parameters being updated.
grad_thetaG a - Remaining derivative through D and G to generator weights.
E_z - Average over latent inputs used in the G objective.

2. Ideal discriminator (balanced real/fake priors)
D*(x) - Optimal discriminator score for fixed generator distribution.
p_data(x) - Density of real data at x.
p_G(x) - Density induced by the generator at x.

3. Conditional GAN objective
V_c - Conditional adversarial value function.
y - Requested condition: label, text representation or image.
D(x,y) - Discriminator score for an observation-condition pair.
G(z,y) - Generator output using both noise and condition.
E_(x,y) - Average over matching real observation-condition pairs.
E_(z,y) - Average over generated noise-condition pairs.

4. Conditioning augmentation
c - Text or other condition representation.
μ(c) - Mean predicted from that condition.
σ(c) - Standard deviation predicted from that condition.
ε - Standard-normal random conditioning noise.
c_hat - Sampled, augmented conditioning vector.
circle-dot (⊙) - Elementwise multiplication of scale and noise.

Convolution sizes and parameter counts
1. Convolution and transposed convolution
H_in - Input spatial height.
H_out - Output spatial height.
k - Kernel size in the height direction.
p - Padding on each side in this direction.
s - Stride: displacement between neighboring kernel positions.
floor brackets - Round the enclosed value down to an integer.
H_out,transpose - Output height of the transposed convolution.

2. Shared convolution weights
k_h, k_w - Kernel height and width.
C_in - Number of input channels.
C_out - Number of output channels/filters.
k_h k_w C_in C_out - Number of shared kernel weights.
final C_out term - One optional bias per output channel.
#conv params - Total trainable convolution parameters.

3. Supporting shape arithmetic
H, W, C - Image height, width and number of channels.
H x W x C - Number of scalar pixel-channel values in an image.
dim(z)+dim(y) - Width of a concatenated noise-condition input.
sum K_j - Width when concatenating one-hot categorical fields.
product K_j - Number of possible combinations of those fields.
K_j - Number of categories in categorical field j.
resolution ratio - At fixed channels, pixel count scales with height x width.

Entropy and Inception Score
1. Entropy and the marginal class distribution
H(P) - Uncertainty of a probability vector P.
P_c - Probability of class c in vector P.
c - Class index.
N - Number of generated images.
i - Generated-image index.
P_ic - Classifier probability of class c for generated image i.
m_c - Marginal class probability: mean of column c.
ln - Natural logarithm; entropy is measured in nats.

2. KL rows, mean and exponentiation
K_i - KL from image i's class vector to the marginal m.
P_ic / m_c - Ratio of image-specific to average class probability.
sum over c - Add probability-weighted contributions across classes.
(1/N) sum_i K_i - Mean KL across generated images.
IS - Inception Score: exponential of that mean KL.
exp - Exponential function with base e.

3. Entropy identity and score bounds
H(m) - Entropy of the marginal predicted class distribution.
H(P_i) - Entropy of the classifier vector for image i.
I(X;Y) - Mutual information between generated image and classifier label.
X - Randomly selected generated image in this identity.
Y - Classifier label sampled from its prediction vector.
C - Number of classifier classes; an upper bound on IS.
P_i=m for all i - Every image has the same prediction distribution.

FID, precision, recall and PSNR
1. Feature mean and covariance
f_i - Feature vector extracted from image i.
N - Number of feature samples; covariance formula needs N>1.
μ_hat - Sample mean of the feature vectors.
Σ_hat (Sigma) - Sample covariance matrix of the feature vectors.
N-1 denominator - Unbiased sample-covariance convention.
T superscript - Transpose, forming an outer product of deviations.

2. FID and its diagonal shortcut
FID - Gaussian feature-moment distance; lower is preferred.
μ_r, μ_g - Mean feature vectors for real and generated images.
Σ_r, Σ_g - Covariance matrices for real and generated features.
||μ_r-μ_g||^2 - Squared Euclidean separation between feature means.
Tr(...) - Trace: sum of the matrix's diagonal entries.
(Σ_r Σ_g)^(1/2) - Matrix square root of the covariance product.
j - Feature-coordinate index in the diagonal shortcut.
v_rj, v_gj - Real/generated feature variances in coordinate j.
sqrt(v_rj), sqrt(v_gj) - Corresponding standard deviations.

3. kNN balls and support membership
S - Reference collection of feature vectors.
u_i - Center of the i-th reference ball.
k - Which nearest OTHER reference neighbor sets the radius.
NN_k - k-th nearest neighbor in the supplied candidate collection.
j != i - Exclude the sample itself by index.
r_k(u_i;S) - Radius of the ball centered at reference point u_i.
a - Query feature vector being tested against the balls.
F(a;S) - Membership indicator: 1 if any reference ball covers a.
exists symbol - At least one reference center must satisfy the test.
||a-u||_2 - Euclidean distance from query a to center u.

4. Two directions and an image metric
R, G - Real and generated feature collections, respectively.
|R|, |G| - Counts of real and generated samples.
g, r - One generated query or one real query.
Precision - Fraction of generated points inside real balls.
Recall - Fraction of real points inside generated balls.
PSNR - Peak signal-to-noise ratio, usually reported in dB.
MAX - Stated maximum clean pixel value, such as 1 or 255.
MSE - Mean squared error in the stated image value scale.
log_10 - Base-ten logarithm, unlike ln used in the other losses.

Diffusion: forward process and aggregate noise
1. Schedule symbols and one-step noising
t - Current diffusion timestep.
β_t (beta) - Variance contributed by fresh noise at step t.
α_t (alpha) - One-step signal variance share: 1-beta_t.
alpha_bar_t - Product of all alphas from step 1 through t.
product symbol (Π) - Multiply the indexed factors together.
x_(t-1), x_t - Noisy observations before and after this forward step.
ε_t - Fresh standard-normal step noise, independent of earlier draws.
q(x_t|x_(t-1)) - Fixed forward transition distribution.
I - Identity covariance matrix for standard Gaussian noise.

2. Direct noising, variance and the aggregate draw
x_0 - Original clean observation.
ε without a step index - Aggregate standard-normal noise in the one-shot formula.
sqrt(alpha_bar_t) - Cumulative clean-signal amplitude coefficient.
sqrt(1-alpha_bar_t) - Aggregate-noise amplitude coefficient.
q(x_t|x_0) - Distribution of timestep-t noise conditioned on clean x_0.
Var(x_t) - Marginal variance across both data and corruption noise.
Var(x_t|x_0) - Conditional covariance for a fixed clean observation.
α_2 β_1+β_2 - Variance of the combined independent two-step noise.
ε - Aggregate draw corresponding to the supplied noisy sample.
s - Index of an incremental noise contribution.
β_s, ε_s - Noise variance and fresh draw introduced at step s.
j - Index of later steps that attenuate that contribution.
product from s+1 to t - Remaining signal multipliers after noise was added.
empty product - Equals 1, including the final step's noise contribution.

Diffusion: schedules and true posterior
1. Linear and normalized cosine schedules
t - Step index, from 1 through T for the linear schedule.
T - Total number of diffusion steps.
β_start, β_end - First and last noise variances of the linear schedule.
f(t) - Unnormalized cosine-squared cumulative signal curve.
s in f(t) - Small cosine offset; the lecture uses 0.008.
f(0) - Normalizer ensuring alpha_bar_0 equals 1.
π/2 - Half-pi angle; use radians.
β_t - Derived noise variance for the cosine schedule.
min(...,0.999) - Cap that prevents a single beta from reaching 1.

2. Posterior when the clean image is known
q(x_(t-1)|x_t,x_0) - True reverse posterior conditioned on clean x_0.
x_t, x_(t-1) - Current noisy observation and previous less-noisy state.
x_0 - Clean observation, known in training but unavailable in generation.
mu_tilde_t - Mean of the true Gaussian posterior.
beta_tilde_t - Variance of the true Gaussian posterior.
α_t, β_t - One-step signal and added-noise variance shares.
alpha_bar_(t-1) - Cumulative signal share before the current step.
alpha_bar_t - Cumulative signal share through the current step.
I - Identity matrix giving isotropic posterior covariance.

Diffusion: reverse sampling and training
1. Clean estimate and reverse mean
x_0_hat - Estimated clean observation implied by predicted noise.
x_t - Current noisy sample available to the network.
ε_theta(x_t,t) - Neural network's prediction of aggregate noise.
θ (theta) - Denoiser parameters learned during training.
μ_theta(x_t,t) - Predicted mean of the next reverse transition.
α_t, β_t - Signal/noise schedule values at the current step.
alpha_bar_t - Cumulative signal variance share.

2. A complete stochastic reverse transition
x_T - Initial Gaussian sample from which generation starts.
T - Final/maximum timestep; sampling runs down to 1.
x_(t-1) - Sample produced by the current reverse transition.
σ_t - Standard deviation of the chosen reverse sampling noise.
z - Fresh standard-normal reverse-sampling draw.
μ_theta - Center of the reverse sampling distribution.
z=0 at t=1 - Omit added noise in the final DDPM step.

3. Noise-prediction training objective
t~Uniform{1,...,T} - Sample a training timestep uniformly.
x_0 - Clean training example used to manufacture x_t.
ε - True aggregate noise used in that manufacture.
ε_theta or ε_hat - Predicted noise from input pair (x_t,t).
L_simple - Expected squared noise-prediction error.
L_MSE - Coordinate-mean version of squared noise error.
D - Number of noise coordinates being averaged.
j - Noise-coordinate index.
∂L_MSE/∂ε_hat_j - Output gradient passed into the denoiser.

Diffusion: the variational bound
1. Bound and its three kinds of terms
L_VLB - Variational upper bound on expected negative data log-likelihood.
E_q - Average under the fixed forward corruption process.
L_T - Endpoint KL comparing the terminal forward noise to the prior.
sum from t=2 to T - Add the intermediate reverse-posterior KL terms.
L_(t-1) - KL between true and learned reverse transitions at that step.
L_0 - Final-step reconstruction negative log-likelihood.
q(x_T|x_0) - Terminal forward distribution for a clean example.
p(x_T) - Chosen terminal prior, usually N(0,I).
q(x_(t-1)|x_t,x_0) - True reverse posterior used as the training reference.
p_theta(x_(t-1)|x_t) - Learned reverse transition distribution.
p_theta(x_0|x_1) - Learned final reconstruction likelihood.

2. Gaussian KL becomes squared mean error
m_q, m_p - Mean vectors of the two Gaussian distributions.
s^2 - Common isotropic variance of BOTH Gaussian distributions.
I - Identity matrix giving the covariance s^2 I.
||m_q-m_p||^2 - Squared Euclidean separation of the mean vectors.
2s^2 - Variance-dependent scaling of the mean mismatch.

3. Weighted noise error and the simplification
C_t - Term independent of denoiser parameters for fixed variances.
w_t - Schedule-dependent weight on timestep-t noise error.
ε, ε_theta - True aggregate and predicted aggregate noise.
β_t - Forward added-noise variance.
σ_t^2 - Fixed reverse-model variance, not standard deviation.
α_t - One-step retained signal variance share.
alpha_bar_t - Cumulative retained signal variance share.
