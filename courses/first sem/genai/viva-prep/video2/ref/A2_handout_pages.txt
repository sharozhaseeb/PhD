Part A: A Small Diffusion Model from Scratch (40%) 
Dataset: Fashion-MNIST, padded to 32 × 32 and scaled to [−1, 1]. Train only on your three classes: d, 
(d + 3) mod 10 and (d + 6) mod 10. Each student trains on three full Fashion-MNIST classes, which is 
18,000 images (6,000 per class). 
Class labels: 0 T-shirt, 1 Trouser, 2 Pullover, 3 Dress, 4 Coat, 5 Sandal, 6 Shirt, 7 Sneaker, 8 Bag, 9 
Ankle boot. 
Rules: you may use diffusers.UNet2DModel as the network. The noise schedules, forward process, 
training loop and sampling loop must be written by you in PyTorch (do not use diffusers schedulers 
or pipelines).

and Dhariwal, 2021).

A2. Training with the ε-prediction loss (15%) 
• Use a small U-Net (suggested: block_out_channels=(32, 64, 128)). Initialise the U-Net 
from a configuration with random weights; do not load pretrained weights (e.g. no 
from_pretrained). 
• Implement the DDPM training step: sample x0, t and ε, build xt, predict εθ(xt, t) and minimise ‖ε 
− εθ‖2. 
• Train two models with identical settings, one with the linear schedule and one with the cosine 
schedule, for about 15 minutes each on Colab. 
• Plot both training loss curves on one figure and save both checkpoints. Report training time, 
batch size, learning rate and number of epochs. 
What to expect: samples will be recognisable and class-like, but not perfect. Some will be 
blurry or odd-looking, especially with less training. Samples need not be perfect; the goal is a 
working DDPM. Recognisable class shapes are sufficient.

Part B: Inside ViT and Swin (40%) 
Dataset (your own): photograph 4 classes of everyday objects around you (for example mugs, shoes, 
books, plants), 25 photos per class, 100 photos in total, taken with your phone. Vary the background, 
angle and lighting. Include a collage of your dataset in the report. These photos must be your own; 
the viva will check this. 
Models: pretrained vit_base_patch16_224 and swin_tiny_patch4_window7_224 from timm. No 
fine-tuning is required. 
Preprocessing your photos: do not resize by hand. Use each model's own timm transform: cfg = 
timm.data.resolve_model_data_config(model) and transform = 
timm.data.create_transform(**cfg, is_training=False). It resizes the shorter side, centre-
crops 224 × 224 and normalises with ImageNet statistics. Load each photo with 
ImageOps.exif_transpose(Image.open(path)).convert('RGB') so phone photos are not 
rotated sideways. Keep the object centred and filling most of the frame, since the centre crop 
removes the edges.

images per model (or all, if fewer). Discuss which backbone gave better features for your data 
and suggest why.
