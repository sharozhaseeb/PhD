# Three-Year Research Plan

## Year 1 — Benchmark and failure map

### Objectives

- complete a systematic novelty and methodology review;
- secure or construct a point-in-time, survivorship-aware dataset;
- formalize the target, regime observables, costs, and evaluation protocol;
- reproduce strong individual models, fixed ensembles, MoE, and AlphaMix-style routing;
- implement masking, placebo signals, frozen folds, and factor attribution; and
- map when existing routers and agents fail.

### Potential publication

An empirical benchmark or failure-analysis paper showing how leakage, regime transitions, attribution, or test reuse alters conclusions about financial agents.

## Year 2 — Selective-routing method

### Objectives

- formalize the delayed-feedback selective-routing problem;
- develop instance-level and strategy-level reliability monitoring;
- develop calibrated abstention and constrained action policies;
- compare deterministic, supervised, mixture, bandit, and agent controllers;
- quantify routing regret and transition-period calibration degradation; and
- investigate a time-uniform risk or regret result under explicit assumptions.

### Potential publication

A methodological paper on delayed-feedback selective routing, including strong non-agent baselines and failure conditions.

## Year 3 — Generalization and prospective validation

### Objectives

- test replacement specialists and an external universe or market;
- run a locked, preregistered shadow-trading study;
- evaluate model/provider drift, costs, factor exposures, and operational failures;
- release deterministic replay artifacts where licensing permits; and
- synthesize benchmark, method, and prospective evidence into the thesis.

### Potential publication

A longitudinal validation and generalization paper, including negative findings if performance does not transfer.

## Novelty protection

- repeat and log the closest-work search every six months;
- keep models replaceable and interfaces stable;
- make the formal problem and evaluation protocol primary contributions;
- maintain an untouched forward period;
- avoid dependence on a single commercial model or API; and
- preserve publishable benchmark and negative-result contributions.
